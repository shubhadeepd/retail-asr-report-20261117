What we tested

14 customer situations × 2 speech presets = 28 conversations. Each situation keeps the same customer goal, but the actual words and conversation path can differ.

Control: added noise and interruptions disabled; telephone audio still applies. Regular: native benchmark settings, which can include noise and interruptions.

Selected from reported retail ASR failures involving names, spelling, emails, digits, negation and background speech. Six scenarios are for development and eight for evaluation, with separate caller identities. This targeted subset does not estimate general retail accuracy. Scenario details appear below.

How WER was measured

1 · CaptureSave the exact audio delivered to the agent’s ASR and its final transcripts.
2 · Check audioGemini transcribes independently, without scripts, expected answers or agent ASR text.
3 · Align & countMatch audio and final ASR by time/IDs; count replaced, missing and extra words.
4 · Keep task scoreSave the benchmark’s separate pass/fail result, tool calls and unmet goals.

WER = (replaced + missing + extra words) ÷ reference words. The pooled rate uses total errors / total words, not the average of conversation percentages. All delivered audio and final ASR events are included, including output during silence; temporary ASR guesses are excluded. Text is lowercased, apostrophes removed, other punctuation replaced by spaces and whitespace collapsed. Names and digits are not corrected; number/email formatting can affect the count.

Supporting investigation

9 failed calls stalled before any tool; 11 failed at identifier lookup; 6 reached account/order data before failing. These are observed stages, not proven causes.

Examples: 088 regular has the email digits in ASR, but the agent adds a dot that breaks lookup. 025 control processes a PayPal return despite ASR saying “Amex … not PayPal”. Offline replay reproduced all 28 database checks. Human audio review and full corrected-ASR conversation tests remain pending. Scoring also needs review: 025 regular passes without an executed transfer/order change; 105’s reference exchange itself fails for insufficient gift-card balance.

What limits the comparison

Human listening is required before calling this golden WER. Machine references are drafts, including the 26 that are complete. Lower WER alone does not prove better task success: reasoning, timing and interruptions also matter.

Runtime repairs were needed for startup dependencies, cancelled tools, assistant TTS and interrupted conversation history. The 28 scored conversations span these runtime versions. 40 total attempts are preserved, including 12 infrastructure failures without task scores; no scored task failure was rerun to seek a pass. Integrity checks passed for every selected case and saved attempt. Audio, final ASR, references, tools and native scores are retained in the source evidence.

Next steps

  1. Cross-check audio references: add a second model family to the existing Gemini checks. Use consistent agreements provisionally; retain disagreements and the two reference gaps as unresolved. This additional verification is proposed, not completed.
  2. Prioritize likely ASR contributions: review the three evidence chains above, including agent recovery errors. Use paired transcript-correction tests to measure task rescues; these are not required to make a qualified trajectory judgment.
  3. Train and compare: build an external language model from separate domain text, then replay identical frozen audio with LM off/on and compare WER. Exclude selected tasks, identities, audio and references from training.
  4. Compare task success fairly: establish a baseline on one stable repaired runtime and hold other components fixed for new conversations. Check other domains for regressions.
